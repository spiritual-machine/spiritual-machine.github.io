Confronting Digital Distractions

Summary

Digital distractions pose a barrier to the uninterrupted attention that many tasks (work, study) require. Our aim was to prototype an intervention to assist in mitigating digital distractions for graduate students and young professionals. To do this, we leveraged primary and secondary research methods and used iterative design processes. The final ideas were evaluated using a scoring matrix.

Details

  • Initial survey – The majority of the respondents (n=48) to our initial survey were graduate students or young professionals between ages of 21-30 reporting 6-11 hours of screen time per day. The survey indicated that notifications and social media were major factors in digital distractions. From this information, we created user personas to guide our research moving forward.
  • Interviews – We conducted two interviews (~30 minutes each) which gave us more insight into the behaviors associated with digital distractions.
  • Literature review – A literature review was carried out using three initial research questions and forming search strings targeted at answering each inquiry.
  • Affinity diagramming –We created an affinity diagram from the data collected from surveys, interviews, and the literature review to synthesize final conclusions.
  • Ideation – Nine ideas were formed with iteration done as obvious areas for improvement were identified.
  • Evaluation – We evaluated each idea using a scoring matrix considering themes such as habit loops, haptic hooks, and novelty.

Final Results

We settled on a final intervention in the form of a charging sleeve. A user would place their phone into a fabric sleeve which allows for the phone to be charged. The sleeve hides the screen, including any notifications.
