Improving the Boba Experience

Summary

Boba tea has become an increasingly popular drink in the United States over the past few decades. Boba tea shops aim to hook customers with novel drinks and a captivating in-store experience. However, the boba tea shop experience has room for improvement, especially around their ordering experience. For this project, we researched ways to improve the user experience of ordering at a kiosk. Using both primary and secondary research methods, we came to the conclusion that the kiosks could likely be improved by offering more privacy. Our final solution involved combining a privacy screen (similar to those used on phones) with a software-based FaceLock technology that would lock the screen temporarily if the customer moves away.

Details

  • Observations – We conducted observations at two local boba tea shops to witness how customers operated the kiosks. We found that the UI was a bit difficult to navigate and friends often were explaining to each other how it worked. We noticed some anxiety about holding up the line or being watched during ordering.
  • Literature review – Our literature review revealed that kiosk ordering anxiety is a real phenomenon which intensifies with closer proximity to strangers and a higher number of strangers nearby.
  • Affinity diagramming – We used an affinity diagram to compile our data together. We found that there were common themes involving anxiety and commercial pressures.
  • Evaluation – We evaluated our final idea by conducting usability testing with three participants. Participants stated that the FaceLock feature especially helps with their feelings of trust towards the kiosk.

Final Results

Our final solution was a hybrid approach between a physical design change (adding a privacy screen) and a software change (adding a timed screen lock when the customer steps away).
